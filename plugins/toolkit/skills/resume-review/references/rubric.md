# Résumé Rubric — evidence-based

Built 2026-09-14 from university career-center, recruiter and ATS vendor sources. Used by the resume-review skill.

Source notes: quotes from the Stanford, CMU, DePaul and Ladders 2012 PDFs and the CareerOneStop pages were read directly (verbatim). The other quotes came through a fetch-extraction tool, so wording may be lightly normalized. Lever's help article and UC Davis's page could not be retrieved, so neither is cited.

## A. Findings a–j

**a) Sections, order, length**
- CMU: "Required Sections: Contact Information, Education, Experience and/or Projects, Skills" … "the most important/relevant content should be listed first." https://www.cmu.edu/career/documents/resources-by-college/scs/scs-resume-guide-2022.pdf
- Harvard: "List headings (such as Experience) in order of importance"; "Within headings, list information in reverse chronological order." https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/resources/create-a-strong-resume/
- MIT CAPD checklist: "You may use two pages if you have an advanced degree or extensive experience (10+ years)." https://capd.mit.edu/resources/resume-checklist/
- Laszlo Bock (ex-Google SVP People): "one page of resume for every ten years of work experience." https://time.com/3617063/biggest-mistakes-resumes-corrections/
- Penn: if you go past one page, "put the most important information on the first page." https://careerservices.upenn.edu/channels/resume/
- **Verdict:** 5+ years but under 10 → one page. Order sections by relevance to the target job.

**b) Bullets: verbs, accomplishments, no-metrics**
- MIT: "Start descriptions with a strong action verb" and "Record accomplishments and contributions, not just responsibilities … 'was responsible for delivering projects on time' → 'ensured projects were delivered on or ahead of schedule.'" https://capd.mit.edu/resources/resumes/
- Bock X-Y-Z: "Accomplished [X] as measured by [Y] by doing [Z]." https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/20140929001534-24454816-my-personal-formula-for-a-better-resume
- CMU: "Action Verb + Context (tell the what) + Result (Metrics, Outcome, and/or Impact)" and "no more than two lines per bullet point."
- **No metrics:** Stanford BEAM C-A-R: "Outcomes of actions (quantify results when possible) - OR - Include the purpose or significance of task/project/goal (why did you do something)." https://careered.stanford.edu/sites/g/files/sbiybj22801/files/media/file/developing_your_resume_handout.pdf
- CareerOneStop (US Dept. of Labor): "include the context of your work, why you did it, for whom, and what the outcome was" and "Include numbers. How many people were served/supervised/informed/helped?" Counts of scope are valid evidence. https://www.careeronestop.org/JobSearch/Resumes/ResumeGuide/SampleResumeWalkThrough/work-experience.aspx
- DePaul: "Use a variety of action verbs." Formula: "Action Verb + Example + Result."

**c) Founders, freelancers, anonymous clients**
- Indeed: "Owner, [Business Name]"; "Summarize all relevant self-employment history in a single entry." https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/self-employed-resume
- Arcadia University: "consolidate projects under a 'Freelance Work' header, categorized by skill or industry … Each entry should briefly describe the project's scope, the problem solved, and the results achieved." It also says "Clearly mention the clients or industries you worked with." https://careerlaunchpad.arcadia.edu/blog/2026/03/20/how-to-format-a-resume-with-freelance-work/
- CareerOneStop's sample uses a "combination style (chronological with functional headings)" with the "same set of task headings" per job. This supports Educate/Consult/Build subheads.
- Bock, on confidentiality (paraphrased): a candidate wrote "Consulted to a major software company in Redmond, Washington" and was rejected. A description that points to an identifiable client still breaches confidentiality. (time.com link above)

**d) Skills**
- CMU: "Sub-categorize skills whenever possible. List skills in order of proficiency … Do not include soft skills such as 'teamwork' or 'leadership'." Its template adds "[List only skills in which you are proficient]", so it uses no rating scales.
- UCSF: never list "communication or leadership in a separate skills section, because you won't be providing the context that makes it believable"; "ok to have your skills represented in both" places. https://career.ucsf.edu/news/should-a-resume-have-a-separate-skills-section
- MIT: listing skills in bullets "reinforces them by putting them in context."
- Workday's parser does not auto-fill "Skills", so key skills must also appear in experience text. https://doc.workday.com/admin-guide/en-us/human-capital-management/recruiting/candidates/set-up-prospects-and-candidates/hdc1552497830785.html

**e) Projects, open source, speaking, community**
- CMU: "Include a select number of … projects … to illustrate practical applications of your skillset." Conference talks go in "a separate section if numerous, or under the relevant … experience."
- Penn: Projects sections are "usually for software engineering and computer science resumes"; "Consider also including your Github link in your header." Publications and presentations: "Don't cite … in full for non-research settings. Summarize instead."
- Pitt: "Extensive or notable work? Create a separate 'Publications' and/or 'Presentations' section." https://careercentral.pitt.edu/resources/listing-publications-presentations-on-your-resume/
- DePaul: projects go "in skill-based experience sections … or in a separate 'Projects' section." https://resources.depaul.edu/career-center/faculty-staff/Documents/How%20to%20Format%20Projects%20on%20Your%20Resume.pdf
- Stanford BEAM lists descriptive headings such as "Teaching Experience", "Training and Development", "Projects" and "Professional Associations".

**f) Dates, gaps, early self-taught experience**
- CareerOneStop: "Be truthful about the dates of your employment." Years-only dates are OK, "However, know that some applicant tracking systems will look for month." "Typically, you should reference the past 10-15 years' work history." https://www.careeronestop.org/JobSearch/Resumes/ResumeGuide/special-tips.aspx
- CMU: "'Experience' as a standalone title implies employment. If the information … is not in fact employment, the section should be renamed."
- Indeed: for self-taught skills, "find other ways to prove your abilities," such as projects, a portfolio or freelance work. https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/self-taught-resume
- Bock: "Putting a lie on your resume is never, ever, ever, worth it."
- **Verdict:** personal, pre-employment automation can be mentioned only when it is labeled as such. It must not be folded into a years-of-experience figure.

**g) Contact line, links, headline**
- CMU: name, email, phone, "LinkedIn URL … links to a personal website, portfolio or GitHub." Do not include a photo or personal data.
- Penn: "City, State/Country … GitHub or personal website to show relevant projects."
- Indeed: a headline is "a short, one-line phrase" placed "directly below your name." "Review the job description and look for keywords … Including these words in your headline will demonstrate why you're a good fit." https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/how-to-write-a-resume-headline

**h) Tailoring for instructor, trainer or teaching roles**
- DePaul Educators guide: experience "is mandatory and the heart of any teaching resume … Include specific lesson plans, classroom management skills and teaching styles." Transferable skills include "training, collaborating or mentoring." "Buzz words are extremely effective when used in moderation … with direct examples to support their claim." https://resources.depaul.edu/career-center/career-advising/advisors/Documents/Resume%20Guide%20for%20Teachers,%20Counselors,%20and%20AdministratorsMAY2014.pdf
- Stanford C-A-R context step: "Who did you work with (team collaborated on and/or population served)."
- U. Arizona: "use the keyword 1-3 times"; "If you do not have a particular experience, it's okay to leave that keyword off." https://career.arizona.edu/resources/tailoring-your-resume/

**i) ATS formatting**
- MIT: use ".doc/.docx or .pdf", common fonts at "10pt or higher", "Avoid use of graphics, icons, or images", "Avoid placing information into tables or text boxes", and no columns. "Avoid abbreviating relevant keywords." Test by saving as .txt. https://capd.mit.edu/resources/make-your-resume-ats-friendly/
- Berkeley: ".5-inch to one-inch margins"; "Avoid using headers, footers, text boxes, tables, colors, pictures, or graphics." https://career.berkeley.edu/prepare-for-success/resumes/
- Indeed: "standard section headings"; "Use both acronyms and full titles." https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/ats-resume-template
- Greenhouse accepts ".doc", ".docx", ".pdf", ".rtf", ".txt". https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/360052218132-Supported-formats-for-resumes-cover-letters-and-other-candidate-uploads
- Workday: "Resume parsing results can vary based on resume format and order of words"; use resumes "that don't have images or image-based styles."

**j) Things that hurt**
- Ladders 2012 (30 recruiters, 10 weeks): recruiters spend "only 6 seconds" and "almost 80%" of review time on name, current and previous title/company, their start and end dates, and education. https://www.theladders.com/static/images/basicSite/pdfs/TheLadders-EyeTracking-StudyC2.pdf
- Ladders 2018: average scan "7.4 seconds". Low performers had "Cluttered layouts characterized by long sentences, multiple columns, and very little white space" and "A reliance on keyword stuffing." https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/ladders-updates-popular-recruiter-eye-tracking-study-with-new-key-insights-on-how-job-seekers-can-improve-their-resumes-300744217.html
- Harvard: "Do not use personal pronouns"; top mistakes include "passive language" and "not demonstrating results"; avoid abbreviations and slang.
- MIT: the "responsible for" example above; avoid vague "various," "multiple," "several," "etc."
- SHRM: a keyword-matched résumé reads like "you're reading the job description"; hiring managers want "real work experience without inflated duties." https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/news/talent-acquisition/ai-written-resumes--use-a-discerning-eye--hiring-managers-advise
- Vanity metrics: no source uses that term. The rule below combines Bock (the measure must be of *your* accomplishment) and CareerOneStop ("What changed as a result of your work?").

## B. Rubric (100 points)

**Scoring:** each check scores 1 (pass), 0.5 (one minor defect) or 0 (fail). For countable checks (for example, the share of bullets that start with a verb), score the proportion. Category score = weight × (points ÷ checks).

**Bands:** A 90–100 · B 80–89 · C 70–79 · D 60–69 · F <60.

**Gate:** a claim that a skimming recruiter would read as materially different from the facts caps the overall grade at C until fixed (Bock; SHRM).

| # | Category | Wt | Pass/fail checks | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Bullet evidence | 25 | E1 every bullet opens with an action verb · E2 no duty phrasing ("responsible for", "duties included") · E3 context: for whom or in what setting · E4 a result: a metric, a scope count, or a stated purpose or outcome · E5 one accomplishment per bullet, ≤2 lines · E6 no verb opens >2 bullets in one group · E7 present tense for current work, past for finished | MIT, CMU, Bock, Stanford C-A-R, CareerOneStop, DePaul |
| 2 | Target fit | 20 | T1 headline carries the target role or its keywords · T2 summary ≤3 lines and specific to this role · T3 the posting's must-have keywords appear 1–3× each, only where true · T4 the strongest role evidence sits in the top third / first sections · T5 teaching bullets name audience, format and topic | Indeed headline, CMU, Arizona, Harvard, Ladders, DePaul Educators, Stanford |
| 3 | Credibility | 15 | C1 year claims match dated professional history, or are labeled personal/self-taught · C2 numbers are internally consistent · C3 numbers measure the candidate's work, not a vendor's catalog or popularity · C4 confidential clients cannot be identified · C5 no typos; consistent punctuation | CareerOneStop, Bock, SHRM, Harvard |
| 4 | ATS parseability | 10 | A1 single column, no tables or text boxes · A2 standard font ≥10pt, margins ≥0.5in · A3 no icon or symbol glyphs that drop out on extraction · A4 plain-text extraction keeps titles, dates, bullets and keywords in order · A5 core sections use standard headings · A6 accepted file type; key acronyms spelled out once | MIT ATS, Berkeley, Indeed, Greenhouse, Workday |
| 5 | Skim layout | 10 | L1 no stranded spill onto an extra page · L2 bold titles with right-aligned dates on every entry · L3 white space; no wall of long lines · L4 every entry of a type carries the same fields | Ladders 2012/2018, CareerOneStop ("Right justify your work dates"), DePaul (consistency), Penn |
| 6 | Structure & length | 5 | S1 core sections present (contact, experience, skills) · S2 reverse-chronological within sections · S3 length fits experience (1 page under 10 years) · S4 headings describe their content; "Experience" means employment | CMU, Harvard, MIT, Bock |
| 7 | Skills | 5 | K1 grouped into categories · K2 tools and hard skills only; no sentences or soft skills · K3 no self-ratings; proficient tools only · K4 key skills also appear in bullets | CMU, UCSF, Penn, MIT, Workday |
| 8 | Projects / OSS / speaking | 5 | P1 a select set, relevant to the target · P2 each entry has name, what it is, the candidate's role and a date · P3 speaking summarized, not cited in full · P4 links point to evidence | CMU, Penn, Pitt, DePaul |
| 9 | Header & contact | 5 | H1 name largest and bold · H2 city/state, phone, professional email · H3 LinkedIn plus GitHub/portfolio · H4 no photo or personal data | CMU, Penn, MIT checklist |
